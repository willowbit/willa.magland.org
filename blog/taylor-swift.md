What is the secret to the peace of old people? Their wisdom? Their faith? I'm used to looking in the lyrics of a select few: the old faces of Bob Dylan and Leonard Cohen, with their dark, layered words. Two years ago I had my [Bob Dylan fall](/blog/2024-10-24/). The leaves are falling again, but to a different voice. The voice is Taylor Swift.

I know two people close to me that love Taylor Swift. I didn't understand it for the longest time. I listened to *Evermore* and it didn't really do much to me. It wasn't the moment, not the [right time to change](/blog/getting-killed/). These days I paint my nails to her ballads, mouth her words while I turn my skirt in the mirror, and sometimes blast *Reputation* when I drive at night. Listening to Taylor Swift makes me happy, but I also think it has tought me something about living well.

One of my favorite songs, on one of my favorite albums, is "State of Grace." The chorus goes:

> Love is a ruthless game
> unless you play it good and right

"*Good* and right"—there's something in the way she says it that makes it clear.  The futility of trying to win a winless game. Playing it good includes it all, and it is all we can do.

What is "good"? What does "living well" mean? Does it mean rearranging all of your furniture? Does it mean nice baskets for the shelf beside your bed? What does a peaceful life look like? It's a life where I saw everything before it came, without surprise, arranged in tupperware and small wooden boxes.  This is the hunger for order that is sold to us online. A final simplicity. All of your mind in one place. A second brain to give the first one a moment of quiet, auto-tagged and backlinked with an AI-summary to lead. As nice as it all looks, it's not quite real life.

> And I never saw you coming
> And I'll never be the same

Moving into my house at the beginning of the summer, I had this image in my head of a woman, sitting at her kitchen table by the window, eating sardines on a big sheet of newspaper. Later I read a poem about a mother eating a tangerine standing, in a long dress, in the morning light of the winter. These images, to me, seemed to be the image of living well. An attention to the in-between, thoughts elsewhere, a body at rest. Bare feet. A mediated mind.

I can't expel the chaos from my life, something I have spent so many years trying to do. The order that I thought would bring peace was never order at all. It was instead a balance between order and disorder, the rhythm within the universe speaks. To recognize the ebb and flow of things and to lean into it. To recognize a chaotic day and, in response, implement a familiar evening. This is what it means to do what feels "right."

Embracing the chaos *does mean* accepting the unexpected. But it also leaves you with an amount of prescience, what I believe the people of Macando called "divine providence:" Recognizing a period of calm allows you to softly expect and accept the current when it comes. To brace for it. And to let it take you.

> This is a state of grace
> This is the worthwhile fight
> Love is a ruthless game
> Unless you play it good and right

Willa
